How to play

You are building a quote. Every deal starts small and your job is to make it bigger without losing it.

Grow the quote adds seats, term, a Success Pack or Odoo.sh. Every one of those is worth real money every month, and every one asks a bit more of the customer.

Ask more than they will carry and they walk. You get nothing, and everything you built goes with them. The number to watch is how much you have asked, out of 21.

Send the quote when you think they will still sign. Whatever they sign is what you bank.

About discounting

A discount does not make the quote better. It buys you room with somebody who has run out of patience, and it costs you every month for the length of the term.

You get one licence discount and one Success Pack discount per quote, and the licence one has a ceiling that depends on the term.

Every discount card prints what it buys before you spend it. Somebody who has dealt straight with you gives a few points back. Somebody who has ground you on price since day one gives a single point, and somebody who has threatened to walk since day one gives nothing at all, because they were always going to ask again.

Discounting every hand lifts your close rate and lowers your book. The board ranks book value, so the best scores come from quotes closed at full price.

How to play

You are the rep. Every hand is one live deal, and your job is the same as it is at work: build a quote that actually covers what the customer needs, then send it.

The two sides

On their side of the table are the reasons they have not signed yet. You only ever see one of them. The rest are face down, and together they add up to a number you are trying to beat.

On your side is your quote. Every piece you add is worth points, and points are the value of what you sold.

Your three moves

  • Grow the quote. Add seats, extend the term, add a Success Pack, add Odoo.sh. Real money, and your number goes up.
  • Give a discount. This does not raise your quote, and it does not make a requirement go away. It only moves the part of their resistance that was about money. If what is stopping them is a migration question or a signature from upstairs, a discount buys you nothing and still costs you every month.
  • Send the quote. You stop building and find out.
  • Double down. Once a hand, at any point. You commit to twice the deal, take exactly one more piece of scope, and the quote sends itself. Twice the contract if they sign, twice the pursuit if they do not. Because you only get the one card it closes fewer deals than playing the hand out, so it is wrong early and right once you are near 21 and one good card finishes the job.

The chip you pick

The chip is the room you walk into. A bigger chip puts you in front of a bigger customer: more seats, more likely to need Custom and Odoo.sh, worth far more if it lands, and a longer committee to talk round. It also costs more to chase. A deal you lose takes the pursuit with it, and on a big deal that is a large number.

So the right moment to send is not the same at every chip. On small deals you can send earlier. On enterprise you have to scope further before it is worth sending, because their bar is higher and losing costs more. Playing a big room with a small room instinct is how a book goes backwards.

How a hand ends

  • Signed. Your quote beat their objections and stayed at or under 21. The contract goes on your book.
  • Not enough. Your quote did not cover what they needed, so they went with someone who scoped it properly. You carry what the pursuit cost. Sending straight away almost always ends here.
  • They stalled. Your quote and their objections landed on the same number. Nobody wins a tie: no commission this quarter, but you did not burn the pursuit either and the deal rolls forward. About a quarter of hands end here, the same way a push works at a blackjack table.
  • They walked. You went past 21. You asked for more than this customer could carry, you lose everything you built, and you still carry what the pursuit cost. Every card prints what it will cost you before you take it, so this one is always avoidable.

About discounts

A discount closes more deals. Give one on every hand and your close rate goes up several points and your book value goes down. That is not a quirk of this table, it is what discounting does, and it is why the board ranks you on what you banked rather than on how many you closed.

Two things have to be true before it is worth giving. Price has to be what is actually holding them back, and the customer has to have earned it. The card tells you how much it buys back before you spend anything: a customer who has dealt straight with you returns three points, one who has threatened to walk since day one returns nothing at all. Give it to that second one and you have paid full price for no ground.

The strongest hands here are the ones that close at full price.

The rules you cannot break

The quote can only do what an Odoo rep can actually do. Multi-year licence discounts have a ceiling by term, one-year deals get none of it, implementation discounts stop at 30 percent, hosting stops at eight workers before it needs a dedicated server, and you cannot take the same concession twice. The Rules tab in the Menu lists all of them.
